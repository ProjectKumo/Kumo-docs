A **subscription** is a URL that points at a list of proxy nodes,
hosted by whoever provides your proxy service. Most providers give it
to you on their dashboard, often labelled "Clash subscription" or
"Clash URL".

Kumo speaks the Clash / Mihomo subscription format. If your provider
gives you a Clash subscription, you can paste it straight in.

## First launch

The first time you open Kumo a small onboarding sheet appears with four
steps:

1. **Welcome** — a quick "hi, this is Kumo".
2. **Command-line tool** — optional; covered in [Power
   User](/docs/power-user/command-line).
3. **Agent skills** — optional; covered in [Power
   User](/docs/power-user/agent-skills).
4. **Done** — you are dropped into the main window.

You can skip the optional steps now and turn them on later from
**Settings**.

## Add your first subscription

<Steps>

<Step>
### Open the Profiles destination
In the sidebar on the left, click **Profiles**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Add a new profile
Click the **+** button (top right). A sheet appears asking for a URL or
a local file.
</Step>

<Step>
### Paste your subscription URL
Paste the URL from your provider into the **URL** field. Give the
profile a name you will recognize — for example "Main" or "Provider
name".
</Step>

<Step>
### Save and let Kumo download
Click **Save**. Kumo downloads the subscription, parses the proxy list,
and shows it in the profile detail pane.
</Step>

<Step>
### Make it active
Right-click the new profile in the list and pick **Set as Active**, or
click it once and use the **Use This Profile** button.
</Step>

</Steps>

You now have a profile, but Kumo is not running yet. Time to start it.

## Start Kumo

Switch to the **Overview** destination (top of the sidebar). The big
status card on the left shows **Stopped**. Click **Start**.

Within a second or two it flips to **Running**. The four cards on the
right come alive:

- **Profile** — shows the active profile and traffic counters.
- **Traffic** — a small live chart of upload and download.
- **System Proxy** — toggles whether macOS sends traffic through Kumo.
- **TUN** — for full-system routing (see [Power User](/docs/power-user)).

## Send your Mac's traffic through Kumo

Starting Kumo on its own only makes the proxy *available* on
`127.0.0.1`. To actually route your everyday browsing through it, flip
**System Proxy** to **On** on the Overview screen.

<Callout type="info">
System Proxy uses the same `networksetup` switch that macOS exposes in
**System Settings → Network**. It does **not** install a VPN
configuration. You can turn it off at any time and macOS reverts
immediately.
</Callout>

Open Safari, visit
[ipinfo.io](https://ipinfo.io) or
[whatismyip.com](https://whatismyip.com), and you should see the
location of the node you picked.
