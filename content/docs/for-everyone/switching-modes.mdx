description: Three outbound modes, what each one does, and when to pick which.
---

Kumo (through Mihomo) has three **outbound modes**. The mode answers
the question "when an app asks for the network, which proxy — if any —
does the traffic go to?".

You can switch modes at any time:

- In the GUI: the **Mode** picker on the Overview screen.
- In the menu bar: under **Mode**.
- Keyboard: `⌘1` (Rule), `⌘2` (Global), `⌘3` (Direct).

## Rule mode (default)

**Use this most of the time.**

In Rule mode, Mihomo looks at every connection and decides based on the
rule list in your profile:

- Connections that match **proxy rules** go through the chosen group.
- Connections that match **direct rules** (often your local network,
  Apple services, China-mainland traffic for some subscriptions) go
  direct.
- Anything else falls through to the **default** rule, which usually
  routes through your main proxy group.

This is the mode subscription providers design their rule lists for.
For day-to-day use, this is what you want.

## Global mode

**Use this when you want everything to go through one node.**

In Global mode, Mihomo ignores rules and sends every connection through
the proxy group you have selected. Local network traffic — your printer,
your home server, Bonjour devices — also goes through the proxy, which
is usually not what you want.

Pick Global when:

- You are debugging and want to bypass rule complexity.
- Your provider explicitly tells you to.
- You actually want everything to look like it is coming from one node
  (some payment flows, some geo-tests).

## Direct mode

**Use this to keep Kumo running but stop proxying.**

In Direct mode, Mihomo sends every connection direct, as if the proxy
was off. The core stays running, the configuration stays loaded, and
you can flip back to Rule or Global in one click.

Pick Direct when:

- You want to quickly test "does this site work without the proxy?".
- You are on a network where the proxy is unnecessary, and you want
  to leave Kumo open without taking the long path of stopping the core.

<Callout type="info">
Direct mode does **not** turn off macOS's system proxy setting. If
**System Proxy** is on, macOS still routes traffic to Kumo — and Kumo
just forwards it direct. If you want apps to bypass Kumo entirely, also
turn the **System Proxy** toggle off.
</Callout>

## Switching mode does not break connections

Mihomo handles mode switches live. Existing connections finish on
whatever proxy they were using; new connections follow the new mode.
You do not need to restart anything.
